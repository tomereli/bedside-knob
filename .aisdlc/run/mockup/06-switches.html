A tap acts. That is the entire card.

Four chips, 112 px across against the old 58 px — nearly four times the area — and each one toggles its own switch on the spot. Nothing here selects anything, opens anything, or changes a mode. The complaint in the issue was a chip wearing a button's clothes; these are buttons.

Two of them are the BATH and CLOSET cards, which stop being cards. A whole carousel position each, for one bit of state, was the wrong price.

Left is left. READ L is switch.khdr_shynh_rshy_kh_shynh_qryh_shml — the left-hand reading light, Masha's side — and READ R is switch.slvn_vknysh_n15_2, key N15-2 on his own panel. The chip on the left of the glass is the lamp on the left of the bed, which is the only mapping a hand can use without reading.

They are labelled by side and not by name because DIM's target page already uses TOMER and MASHA for the two nightstand strips. Two different lights called TOMER on adjacent cards is a bug waiting for a dark room.

A lit switch gets a lit border and a lit background — the border alone was too quiet across a dark room. A tap that lands on the bare face between chips fires the refusal buzz rather than doing nothing: a gesture that misses should feel like a miss, not like a dead knob. Hold is the same refusal, as on SCENES.
